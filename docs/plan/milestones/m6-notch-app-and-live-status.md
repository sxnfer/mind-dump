# M6: Notch app and live status

> Read this when: you are working on M6, or need its tasks and done-when list.

**Goal:** the full notch experience.
**Decisions needed:** D11, D23.
**Tasks:**
- [x] The island in SwiftUI: idle, peek (pointer over the notch), compact (a status pill) and expanded (the panel), with one black body whose width, height and corner each follow their own spring (slice 1, `app/MindDump/Island/`). The springs are islandcn's measurements of the iPhone's island (`IslandMotion.swift`). Not yet: split (two activities at once).
- [x] Respect the system "Reduce motion" setting: the shape eases for 0.2 s with no overshoot, and content cross-fades with no blur or scale (slice 1).
- [x] Live status per item, by polling (D11): the pill goes "Saved", "Reading page", "Ready", or says the page could not be read (slice 1). Server-Sent Events wait until the island shows several items.
- Left panel: search, filter chips, list. Right panel: overview. Library and + buttons.
- Global shortcut to open the panel.
- [x] One-box capture, typed or pasted (slice 1): one input sorts a web address into a link and anything else into a note, in the app, and says which before Return. Not yet: dropping a file. One-box capture: one input that takes anything (type, paste or drop) and sorts it for the user into a note, link or file. It replaces M1's clipboard check and its "Write a note instead" choice. Open design question for San: sort in the app or behind the API? (Raised by San on 2026-11-20 after testing M1; parked until after 26 Nov, because the proof track is backend only.)
- [x] Ask from the island (slice 2, branch `m6-ask`, `app/MindDump/Ask/`): the panel has two modes, Capture and Ask, with a pill that slides between them (Tab or a click). Ask streams `POST /ask?stream=true` (Server-Sent Events): a status line ("Thinking", "Searching “…”", "Reading an item"), then the answer at a steady pace with `[c:…]` markers shown as small numbers, then numbered sources that expand to show the passage. Follow-ups continue the conversation; ⌘N or "New question" starts a new one. Closing mid-answer cancels it (the backend saves nothing). Errors (backend down, no key, daily cap, rate limit, an error mid-stream) stay in the panel in plain words. Not from your items: a clear notice and no sources. Debug builds can play canned answers (`MD_ASK_FIXTURE=1`), so the UI was built and measured without paying for model calls.
- [x] Measure smoothness (slice 1): debug builds time every frame of every transition and print the late ones (`Support/Debug.swift`); `MD_MOTION_SCALE=8` gives slow motion, and the app can film its own window. Measured on a 120 Hz display: no late frame in any transition, except one late frame about 240 ms into the first open after launch (AppKit's first text focus). Instruments not used yet.
- Not yet after slice 2: older turns of a conversation (the panel shows only the latest question and answer), Discuss from a source row, search without asking, and the "open" arrow on link sources was not clicked in testing. Measured on a 120 Hz display with debug builds (an idle panel here shows 0 to 2 late frames per 1.3 s on its own): switching modes with a short or no answer, opening and closing a source row, the sources coming in and reopening a long answer show 0 or 1 late frame. Not yet clean: a stream shows a few single late frames (about one every 2 s, including one when the first words appear); switching away from, or closing, a long answer drops 1 to 3 frames; closing while an answer streams makes the collapse's first frame 8 to 21 ms late (see the known issue below); typing drops about one frame per key in both modes; the first open after launch still has its one late frame (AppKit's first text focus).
- Known issue (slice 2): closing while an answer streams. It was 13 to 38 ms late in the collapse's first frame. Two causes were found and fixed: the text kept flowing under the blur-out, and the panel put the last answer back in that same frame. Now the reveal stops before the collapse starts, and the restore waits 0.7 s, until the panel has faded out (its one late frame now falls where nothing moves). What is left is 8 to 21 ms in the first frame, against 8 to 10 ms for closing a finished answer. Not yet explained; the next things to look at are the request's cancellation and the spinner still turning under the blur.
- Fixed in slice 2 after review: under a long answer the sources came in below the visible part without the view following them, and clicks on them did nothing until the user scrolled. The cause: an animated change made from outside SwiftUI's own update (`withAnimation` from a queued call) left the scroll view's geometry stale, so its measured height, its bottom fade and its hit-testing all kept the old position. Now those changes are plain assignments and the views animate themselves, the view follows the sources in (unless the reader had scrolled up), and an opened row scrolls fully into view.
- Fixed after San tested (2026-11-25): the body went off-centre while its width sprang. The right edge overshot while the left edge crawled, up to 10 points behind for a quarter of a second. The cause: an animated `frame(width:)` is laid out once at its final width, and SwiftUI then animates the size and the position separately; the size took the edge spring, the position took the slower carrier spring. Now an `Animatable` modifier feeds each in-between width (and height) to layout on every frame (`IslandWidth`, `IslandHeight` in `IslandView.swift`). Measured from filmed frames: left and right are equal in every frame of hover, open, close, the pill and its dismissal, still at 120 Hz.
- [x] Drop-and-save, text only (slice 3, branch `m6-drop-text`, `app/MindDump/Capture/DropModel.swift`): drag highlighted text (or a link from a browser) onto the notch and let go. While the drag is over it, the island opens out and says "Let go to save this note" or "this link", with the note's first words or the link's host and path under the notch. A release saves at once, with no click and no key, through the same code as typed capture (`CaptureModel.store`): "Saved", then the live status, or "Already saved". A drag that leaves or is cancelled saves nothing, and the island goes back to what it showed (a running pill comes back; a status that arrived meanwhile shows then). A failed save opens the panel in Capture with the text in the box and the reason under it, so Return retries. Files, images and blank text are refused (the drag flies back). An open panel is never a drop target: its box takes a drop of text as any macOS text field does (it inserts the text where you let go), in Capture and in Ask. A click elsewhere now closes the panel on release and only if the pointer did not travel, so starting a drag in another app no longer closes it.
- How the drag reaches the island: the window ignores the mouse until the pointer is on the island, and a window that ignores the mouse gets no drags. The pointer tracker's global monitor already watches `leftMouseDragged`, so it flips the window to take the mouse when a drag reaches the notch; AppKit then calls the drop view. While the target shows, the hot zone is the grown body plus 24 points, so it does not flap. A small view of our own sits in front of the SwiftUI content and takes the drops (`DropCatcher` in `NotchWindow.swift`), because the panel's text box keeps its focus while closed, and its hidden editor, which takes text drops, sits right where the target opens. (Letting go of the focus on close was tried and measured: it made the first frame of every later open about 15 ms late.) If AppKit never reports the drag leaving, a watchdog that looks at the mouse button five times a second, only while the target shows, takes it down.
- Not verified in slice 3: a real drag from another app. Everything below the AppKit calls was driven by debug script verbs that call the same model methods (`drag`, `dragexit`, `drop`, `dragtypes`). Only a real drag runs: the pasteboard reading (`DropModel.payload(from:)`), the `DropReceiving` adapter, the `DropCatcher` and host overrides, the watchdog, and the click-on-release rule. Unknowns until then: that the global monitor fires during another app's drag (other notch apps rely on it), that flipping the window to take the mouse mid-drag makes AppKit call the drop view, and which pasteboard types browsers put on an image drag (a file-promise drag is refused by name). Measured on a 120 Hz display with debug builds: entering the target 0 late frames (13 of 13 runs), leaving it 0 (8 of 8), the release 0 in 4 of 5 runs and one 8 ms late frame in the other; filmed in slow motion, left and right edges are equal in every frame of enter, leave and release, and Reduce Motion eases with no overshoot.
- Known issues (slice 3): a 20,000-character drop that fails (422) opens the panel with all of it in the box, and that open drops about 13 frames while the field lays the text out. The script's `click` from a bare notch misses (the island's button is 1 point tall until the peek grows it); scripts must `pointer notch` first, as a real pointer does.
