# Field notes: mangrove project MD-2291

Project MD-2291 is a fictional example. It restores mangroves along a stretch of coast where shrimp ponds replaced the original forest decades ago. The developer reopens tidal channels so that seawater reaches the abandoned ponds again, and local crews plant seedlings where natural regrowth is too slow.

The first verification covered 2023 and 2024 and confirmed 18,450 tonnes of removals, of which 2,770 went to the buffer pool, a deduction of fifteen percent set by the project's risk rating. The remaining credits were issued in one batch, and most were sold forward to a single buyer before issuance.

Survival of planted seedlings was the main problem in the first year. Crews planted in the dry season, when the young trees were exposed to heat and to barnacles, and only about forty percent survived. After moving planting to the start of the rainy season and planting closer to existing trees, survival rose above seventy percent.

Community agreements cover fishing rights inside the restored area. Families who gave up shrimp ponds receive a share of credit revenue and keep the right to collect crabs and fish by hand, while new ponds and cutting are banned. A community committee meets every quarter to review the revenue accounts and to report any cutting to the developer.

Monitoring uses drone surveys twice a year and twenty permanent sample plots, where crews measure the trunk diameter and height of every tree. The drone images map the area covered by mangroves, and the plot measurements turn that area into tonnes of carbon.

The next verification is planned for the end of 2026. The developer expects a smaller issuance than the first, because the fastest-growing trees were counted early and the newest plantings will take several years to store much carbon.
