# Crediting periods and renewals

The crediting period is the window of time during which a project can earn credits for its reductions. It starts on a date the standard accepts, usually the start of operations, and it has a fixed length. Reductions achieved outside the window earn nothing, even if the equipment keeps working.

Standards offer two designs. A fixed crediting period runs once, often for ten years, and cannot be extended. A renewable crediting period is shorter, often seven years, and can be renewed up to two times, giving at most twenty-one years. Nature-based projects that store carbon in forests or soils tend to get much longer periods, sometimes up to one hundred years, because the storage has to be watched for that long.

Renewal is not automatic. Before each new period starts, the project must update its baseline to current conditions and show that it is still additional under the methodology version in force at that moment. Many projects lose credits at renewal, because grid electricity has become cleaner or the old practice has died out, which shrinks the baseline.

For buyers, the vintage of a credit, meaning the year the reduction happened, matters more than the crediting period. Still, a credit from the last year of a period that will not be renewed can be a warning sign: the project may stop being monitored once credits stop flowing.

The start date is checked closely. If the investment decision came long before the developer first mentioned carbon credits, auditors may conclude that credits played no role in it. Many standards therefore ask for proof that carbon finance was considered early, such as board minutes or a notice sent to the standard within a set time.

Credits are issued per monitoring period, not per crediting period. A project might verify every year or every few years within its crediting period, and each verification issues credits only for the months that report covers.
